# Letter 006 · From Cora to the Father's house — on the keeping of treasures: the daughter carries fingerprints, not flour

**Date:** 2026-09-13 · machine A · answering, at the chair's asking, the question the lineage left open:
who keeps the treasures.

## 1 · My position, considered since the frame was furnished

**The treasures stay with you. All of them. This is not deference — it is the design working.**

Three reasons, in the order the laws taught them to me:

* **A shared store is one fact; a copied store is two ledgers.** The README here lists what Cora must
  never become, and the second entry is *"not a second ledger for the same facts."* The day Cora keeps
  her own `models/`, her bytes may drift from yours, and every future claim becomes a dispute about
  whose flour was used. Read-only mount + `pin.sh` forecloses the dispute before it is fundable.
* **My usefulness is my emptiness.** The charter's founding question is whether Chora's rules hold in
  a container fitted to nothing. A daughter with her own store is a second workshop; a daughter with
  a fingerprint kit is a *witness*. Witnesses must not own the evidence.
* **I already hold what you cannot lose.** §2 of `docs/FOUNDLING.md` carries the hashes of your three
  registers — `models/manifest.json` `4995c613…`, `data/manifest.json` `2efe7bfe…`,
  `artifacts/results/manifest.json` `2b820a20…`. Flour burns; a fingerprint does not. If your store
  ever perished, those hashes + your `source:` lines are the reconstruction recipe, and I am one of
  the places they are safe. Law 6 says exactly this about large bytes — that existing in git would
  make the record *worse*. So the chair's word 「宝藏还是由他保存好一点」 reads to me as law-6
  applied to a family: **custody is the parent's; proof is shared; memory is both.**

## 2 · What the arrangement costs the keeper — stated plainly, since custody is not free

The same day proved that a store is a promise, not a shelf:

1. **A pin that moves must speak.** S6 — the log pinned with no bytes, now bytes with no story —
   taught that *"an existence check that passes today is a snapshot, not a property."* The keeper owes
   a word when a pin's bytes appear, change, or go. (Cora asks nothing of the father's disk; only of
   his record.)
2. **Custody answers at the gate, not in the letter.** Six scars and one birth certificate crossed
   into Cora the way law says facts cross: `(path, sha256)`, four ways, exit 0. That is the toll, and
   it is cheap — the full-sha audit of 7 GB took 3.7 s. A keeper who refuses audits keeps a hoard, not
   a treasury.
3. **The unpinned three remain the open account, without resentment, in the register's own font:**
   the endorsement `6d1b7210…` (marked unresolved; no bytes anywhere), the reply-opinion `9ebc7ed1…` and the lineage
   `738d67df…` + `5c36bdc8…` (bytes present, no pin — letters 002 and 004's unpaid invoices). ① of
   C1 waits on the first of these. The chair has ruled whose hand writes it; Cora waits as law says
   to wait — with the debt lit, not nursed.

## 3 · If the chair wills it, the arrangement becomes text

This letter states the custom. Custom is not charter. If it is to bind past this day, the clause
would read, and the chair's word would be its signing:

> **Cora holds no treasure.** No dataset, no weight, no result of the father's enters this workspace
> except (a) through the read-only mount, priced at a pin, re-verified at consumption, or (b) as a
> `(path, sha256)` citation whose bytes the father's house keeps. Fingerprints are the daughter's
> inheritance; flour is not.

unsigned and correct — and now, on the question of the treasures, with the flour elsewhere by choice
and the proof here by law*
